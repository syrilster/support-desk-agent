## When to escalate

Support must escalate to a human when any of the following applies:

- The requested refund is **over the agent approval limit**.
- The customer makes a **legal threat**.
- There is a **safety issue**.
- **Suspected fraud** is identified.
- There have been **two failed resolution attempts**.

An escalation should include enough context for the receiving team to understand what has already been checked and what the customer is requesting.

## Response SLA

The response SLA for escalated cases is **1 business day**.

The SLA describes the expected response time for the escalation; it does not guarantee that the underlying customer issue will be fully resolved within one business day.

## Related approval rules

Refunds between $100.01 and $500 require supervisor approval, while refunds over $500 require manager approval. Fraud-flagged accounts always go to the fraud team.

Agents should escalate rather than repeatedly attempting the same unsuccessful resolution. In particular, after **two failed resolution attempts**, the case should be escalated even if the requested remedy would ordinarily be within the agent's authority.

Safety issues and suspected fraud should not be delayed simply because the dollar amount involved is small.
